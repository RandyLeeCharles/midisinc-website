# About the MIDI SINC Project

## Preserving Organ Building Through Open Sharing and Modern Technology

Hello, my name is Randy Charles, and I am the creator of the MIDI SINC Project.

MIDI SINC was created from a simple idea: the knowledge, designs, and technology needed to build and restore pipe organs should be available to everyone, not just those with access to expensive equipment, specialized tooling, or decades of experience.

This project combines traditional organ-building principles with modern technologies including 3D printing, CAD design, custom electronics, MIDI control systems, and open-source software.

The goal is not simply to build a single instrument. The goal is to develop and freely share designs, techniques, and information that can help others explore the fascinating world of organ building.

---

## Who This Project Is For

MIDI SINC is intended for:

- Hobbyists
- Makers
- Experimenters
- Educators
- Students
- Woodworkers
- Restorers
- Organ Builders
- Mechanical Music Enthusiasts

Whether you want to build a complete organ, print a single pipe, restore an existing instrument, explore acoustic design, or simply learn how organs work, this project is intended to provide useful resources and practical information.

---

## Traditional Craft Meets Modern Manufacturing

While much of the project focuses on 3D-printed organ pipes, the objective is not to replace traditional organ building.

Many of the pipe designs developed through this project can be built using either:

- Modern 3D printing methods
- Traditional woodworking methods

using the same dimensional drawings and design principles.

A builder with a desktop 3D printer can produce a pipe directly from the supplied files, while a woodworker can construct the same pipe using proven traditional techniques.

The intention is to provide options, not replace craftsmanship.

---

## Why 3D Printing?

3D printing allows organ pipe designs to be developed, tested, modified, and shared more quickly than ever before.

New ideas can be designed in CAD, printed, tested, tuned, and revised in a matter of hours.

This makes experimentation more affordable and allows hobbyists and makers to participate in organ building without requiring a fully equipped woodworking shop.

By combining traditional acoustic principles with modern manufacturing methods, it becomes possible to explore new approaches while preserving the musical character of traditional pipe organs.

---

## Building the World's First Open-Source 3D-Printed Pipe Organ

One of the primary goals of this project is the development of what may become one of the world's first fully 3D-printed pipe organs.

This effort includes:

- Pipe design and testing
- Acoustic research
- Custom MIDI controller development
- Open-source firmware
- PCB design
- Mechanical systems
- Documentation
