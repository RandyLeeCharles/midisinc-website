About the MIDI SINC Project

Combining Traditional Organ Building with Modern Technology

Hello, my name is Randy Charles, and I am the creator of the MIDI SINC Project.

MIDI SINC was founded on a simple idea: the knowledge, designs, electronics, and technology needed to build and restore mechanical musical instruments should be available to everyone.

This project combines traditional organ-building principles with modern technologies including 3D printing, CAD design, custom electronics, MIDI control systems, printed circuit board development, and open-source software.

The goal is not simply to build a single organ. The goal is to create and freely share designs, techniques, electronics, software, and documentation that can help hobbyists, makers, experimenters, educators, woodworkers, restorers, and future organ builders.

What Is MIDI SINC?

While the project is often described as a 3D-printed pipe organ project, MIDI SINC is actually the development of an entire open-source organ system.

Major development areas include:

  • 3D-printed organ pipe development
  • Traditional organ pipe design
  • Custom MIDI player systems
  • MIDI Solenoid Drivers (MSDs)
  • Controller electronics
  • PCB design and testing
  • Firmware development
  • Musical instrument automation
  • Glockenspiel control systems
  • Mechanical actuator systems
  • Open-source documentation

The objective is to create a complete system that can be used to operate pipe organs, glockenspiels, and other mechanical musical instruments while making the designs available to the community.

Whether your interest is woodworking, electronics, software development, 3D printing, acoustics, or simply learning how mechanical musical instruments work, MIDI SINC is intended to provide useful resources and practical information.

Traditional Craft Meets Modern Manufacturing

An important goal of the project is combining proven organ-building methods with modern manufacturing technology.

Although MIDI SINC includes extensive use of 3D printing, the project is not intended to replace traditional woodworking techniques.

Many of the pipe designs developed through this project can be constructed either as 3D-printed pipes or as traditional wooden pipes using the same dimensions and design principles.

This allows builders to choose the construction method that best fits their tools, skills, and interests.
