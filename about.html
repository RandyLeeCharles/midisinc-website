About the MIDI SINC Project

Preserving Organ Building Through Open Sharing and Modern Technology

Hello, my name is Randy Charles, and I am the creator of the MIDI SINC Project.

MIDI SINC was created from a simple idea: the knowledge, designs, and technology needed to build or restore pipe organs should be available to everyone, not just those with access to expensive equipment, specialized tooling, or decades of experience.

This project combines traditional organ-building principles with modern technologies including 3D printing, CAD design, custom electronics, MIDI control systems, and open-source software.

The goal is not simply to build a single instrument. The goal is to develop and freely share designs, techniques, and information that can help others explore the fascinating world of organ building.

Who This Project Is For

MIDI SINC is intended for:

  • Hobbyists
  • Makers
  • Experimenters
  • Educators
  • Students
  • Woodworkers
  • Restorers
  • Organ Builders
  • Mechanical Music Enthusiasts

Whether you want to build a complete organ, print a single pipe, restore an existing instrument, explore acoustic design, or simply learn how organs work, this project is intended to provide useful resources and practical information.

Traditional Craft Meets Modern Manufacturing

While much of this project focuses on 3D-printed organ pipes, the objective is not to replace traditional organ building.

Many of the pipe designs developed through this project can be built using either modern 3D-printing methods or traditional woodworking methods using the same dimensional drawings and design principles.

A builder with a desktop 3D printer can produce a pipe directly from the supplied files, while a woodworker can construct the same pipe using proven traditional techniques.

The intention is to provide options, not replace craftsmanship.

Why 3D Printing?

3D printing allows organ pipe designs to be developed, tested, modified, and shared more quickly than ever before.

New ideas can be designed in CAD, printed, tested, tuned, and revised in a matter of hours rather than weeks.

This makes experimentation more affordable and allows hobbyists, makers, and educators to participate in organ building without requiring a fully equipped woodworking shop.

Building the World's First Open-Source 3D-Printed Pipe Organ Pipes (as printing the Windchests are beyond desktop pinters size capacity)

One of the primary goals of this project is the development of what may become one of the world's first fully 3D-printed pipe organs.

Development efforts include:

  • Pipe design and testing
  • Acoustic research
  • Custom MIDI controller development
  • Open-source firmware
  • PCB design and testing
  • Mechanical systems
  • Documentation and educational resources

Every component is developed with the intention of sharing the results and helping others learn from both the successes and the challenges encountered along the way.

Open Source Commitment

A core principle of the MIDI SINC Project is that knowledge should be shared.

As designs are tested and validated, resources will be released whenever possible, including:

  • Pipe drawings
  • Dimensional plans
  • STL files
  • CAD models
  • Electronics schematics
  • PCB layouts
  • Firmware
  • Software
  • Construction notes
  • Testing results
  • Lessons learned

The objective is to build a growing library of resources that future builders can use, modify, improve, and expand.

Why I Continue

Over the years I have personally invested thousands of dollars and countless hours into researching, designing, testing, and refining these systems.

As a retiree, progress is sometimes limited by both time and budget, but the project continues moving forward because I believe these resources can help others enter a hobby that has traditionally been difficult to access.

Every successful design, every tested pipe, and every lesson learned becomes another piece of information that can be shared with the community.

Looking Ahead

The future of MIDI SINC is not just one organ.

The vision is a growing collection of freely available designs, software, electronics, construction methods, and educational resources that can benefit generations of hobbyists, makers, experimenters, educators, woodworkers, and organ builders.

Thank you for visiting and following the project.

I hope the information shared here inspires you to build, experiment, learn, and help keep the art of organ building alive.

Randy Charles
Founder, MIDI SINC Project
